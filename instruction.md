You are a Product Manager on the Growth Team at the product you have chosen.
Starting a SIP is easy. A few taps, a small monthly amount, and a quiet promise to your future self.

Staying with it is a different matter. Months pass, life gets busier, the first few statements look underwhelming, and the SIP that felt like a good decision in month one becomes something users stop thinking about, pause, or let lapse.

One of your company’s strategic goals is to:

Increase the percentage of first-time mutual fund investors who still have an active SIP 12 months after starting their first one.

An active SIP means at least one successful instalment in the last 30 days. Investors who stay invested longer are worth more to the platform and, more importantly, are the ones who actually benefit from investing.

However, you are not being given the underlying user problem. Your task is to discover it. And for the solution, the constraints are that you CANNOT recommend specific funds or stocks, and you CANNOT use fear, FOMO or urgency-based nudges.

Part 1: Build an AI-Powered Discovery Engine
Before proposing any solution, you must build an AI-powered system that analyzes user feedback at scale.

You may use:

Claude
GPTs
Agents
Workflows
n8n
Zapier
Perplexity
Any AI-native stack of your choice
The goal is to analyze sources such as:

App Store reviews
Play Store reviews
Reddit discussions from Indian investing communities
Investing forums such as TradingQ&A
Social media conversations
YouTube comments on personal finance videos
Other publicly available conversations about SIPs and first-time investing in India
Your discovery engine should help answer questions such as:

Why do people start a SIP, and what do they expect to happen?
What changes in their behaviour in the months after starting?
What leads investors to pause, reduce or stop a SIP?
What do investors check, and how often, once they have started?
What confuses or worries them inside the app?
What do they do outside the app when they feel unsure?
What separates investors who continue from those who quietly drop off?
How do these patterns differ across user segments (income stability, age, source of money, who influenced them)?
What unmet needs emerge consistently across conversations?
Your workflow should go beyond summarizing reviews or performing sentiment analysis. It should enable you to identify, quantify where possible, and compare potential opportunity areas that could influence the stated business metric.

You should demonstrate how your workflow gathers and analyzes data, how themes are identified, how insights are generated, and how you validated the quality of the insights (see the AI Evidence Log under Deliverables).

Part 2: Break Down the Business Metric
Break down:

12-month SIP continuation for first-time investors

into the relevant product outcomes and user behaviors that could influence it.

Develop your own decomposition based on your understanding of the product and the evidence from your discovery engine.

Use this decomposition alongside the output of your discovery engine to determine where the highest-potential opportunities lie.

Part 3: Validate the Opportunity Through User Research
AI-generated insights are only a starting point.

Choose a target user segment and opportunity area based on your analysis. Conduct 5 to 6 user interviews with respondents belonging to the segment you have chosen.

Think carefully about the research methodology you would choose.

Understand:

Why they started, and what they expected from it
What the experience has been since
Whether they have ever paused, reduced or considered stopping, and what led to it
What they check and when, and how they feel about what they see
What they do outside the app when they are unsure
What would make them more likely to continue
Do not collect account numbers, PAN details or portfolio screenshots from respondents.

Part 4: Define the Problem
Based on your discovery engine and primary research, clearly articulate:

Your target user segment
The moment or scenario in the first 12 months you are solving for
The product outcome you intend to influence
The root cause
Existing user workarounds
Why solving the problem creates meaningful user value
Why solving the problem makes business sense
Show how your thinking evolved across:

Business Metric -> Product Outcomes -> AI Discovery -> Primary Research -> Problem Definition

Show how your primary research validated or challenged what your discovery engine surfaced.

DO NOT frame the problem simply as:

“Investors stop their SIPs when markets fall.”

Your research should uncover why continuation breaks down for the segment you chose, despite the investor having started with good intent.

Part 5: Build and Deploy an AI-Native MVP
Based on the problem you identify, design, build and deploy a functional MVP. It may take the form of:

A feature within the product you have chosen
An AI-powered workflow
An AI agent
A standalone experience connected to the investing journey
Your MVP must be built around an eval-gated AI feature. It must include a visible quality check or a safe fallback (for example, declining to answer, expressing uncertainty, or pointing to an official source), and you must measure the quality of its outputs against the golden set from your Evidence Log. Explain where in the user journey AI genuinely earns its place, and what a non-AI version would miss.

Your MVP must not present anything as investment advice and should make that clear to users.

You must deploy the MVP to production so another person can use it to attempt a real task. Mentors will test it.

Part 6: Test Your MVP With Users
Return to at least 3 users from your target segment and ask them to use your MVP on a real task. Document what you learned and what you would change in the next iteration.

Part 7: Define Success
Start with the business metric. Then define the metrics your solution would influence: leading indicators, guardrail metrics, the definition of each, and your rationale for choosing them. Include how you would measure the quality of your AI feature’s outputs.

Part 8: Risks & Mitigation Steps
Think about why your solution might fail. Identify the most important risks for your specific solution and propose mitigation plans.
